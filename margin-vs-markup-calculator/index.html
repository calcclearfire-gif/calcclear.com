Margin vs. Markup — what's the difference?

Margin is profit ÷ selling price. Markup is profit ÷ cost. A 50% margin means you keep half the revenue. A 50% markup on a $50 cost means you sell at $75 — only a 33% margin.

Which should you use?

Retailers and service businesses often prefer markup because it's simpler to calculate mentally — "I mark everything up 30%" is easy to apply at the register.

Business analysts and investors prefer margin because it tells you what percentage of revenue you actually keep — more useful when comparing businesses of different sizes.

Margin = (Selling Price − Cost) ÷ Selling Price × 100
Markup = (Selling Price − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100
Selling Price = Cost ÷ (1 − Margin) = Cost × (1 + Markup)

How it works

Margin and markup are two ways of expressing the same relationship — the gap between your cost and your selling price — but they are not the same number. Using one when you mean the other is one of the most common pricing mistakes in retail and wholesale businesses.

Margin   = (Selling Price − Cost) ÷ Selling Price
Markup = (Selling Price − Cost) ÷ Cost

A 50% markup gives you a 33% margin. A 50% margin requires a 100% markup. This calculator solves for the selling price given either metric you prefer to work with.

Know your margin, not just your markup

Your margin is what matters for profitability because it tells you what fraction of each dollar of revenue you keep. A product with 10% markup might sound small — but if the margin is 9%, you keep 9 cents of every dollar. Track margin; use markup as a pricing shortcut.

Margin vs. Markup: The Difference That Costs Businesses Money

If you have been in business for any length of time, you have heard both terms used interchangeably. They are not interchangeable, and confusing them is one of the most common and costly pricing errors in retail, wholesale, and manufacturing businesses.

Margin is the fraction of your selling price that is profit. Markup is the fraction of your cost that you add on top to arrive at your selling price. They start from different bases and give you different numbers. A 50% markup and a 50% margin are not the same thing — they never have been, and they never will be.

Why This Matters for Profitability

Consider a product with a $50 cost. You apply a 50% markup: $50 cost + $25 markup = $75 selling price. Your margin is ($75 − $50) ÷ $75 = 33.3%. You keep 33 cents of every dollar of revenue as profit.

Now apply a 50% margin instead: you keep $0.50 of every dollar, so selling price = $50 ÷ 0.50 = $100. Same "50%" label, very different financial outcomes.

The confusion is especially dangerous when you are trying to hit a target margin. If your business requires 40% margin to cover overhead and generate profit, but you price at 40% markup, you are actually achieving approximately 29% margin — and likely operating at a loss without realizing it.

Which Should You Use?

Use margin as your primary metric because it directly tells you your profitability — what percentage of revenue you keep. Use markup as a quick pricing input when you know your cost and want a rough selling price, but always verify the resulting margin before finalizing your prices.
